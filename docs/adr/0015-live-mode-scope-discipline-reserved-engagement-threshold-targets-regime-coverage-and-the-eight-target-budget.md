# Live-mode scope discipline: reserved engagement-threshold targets, regime coverage, and the eight-target budget

The 2026-09-03 re-run — the first Live run after the operative-provisions and scope-resolution fixes — showed the pipeline still failing scope discipline in four systematic ways, so the coverage metrics understate real answer quality and Answers carry claims that cannot engage in the Scenario's actual facts. Definitions and perimeters are presupposed, never cited: the Planner plans duty areas only, so the provisions that decide whether a regime engages — the GDPR's breach definition, the AI Act's profiling and role definitions, DORA's financial-entity perimeter, the principles constraining the contested processing — are systematically absent from Answers, and in one case a single missed definitional provision was ~93% of the case's recall loss. A Regulation the case names goes unresearched: in the cloud-attack case the Planner spent its whole budget on one regime and produced zero data-protection content despite the question naming the processing of client personal data — ~65% of that case's recall loss. The vendor-side branch drifts: the GPAI model-provider duty area (documentation, downstream information, policies) was missed on both generative cases while the Answers produced whichever provider-side chunks retrieval happened to surface — exactly the residual risk ADR-0014 flagged and deferred to a re-check. And Scenario-excluded branches survive verification: full high-risk deployer regimes were developed for a chatbot the Scenario settles into the transparency tier, a provider serious-incident regime was developed for a firm that is not a provider, and conditional contingencies the Scenario's facts exclude kept rating moderate instead of unsupported. Separately, the declared six-target Planner budget demonstrably strains Scenarios implicating three Regulations.

One batched scope-discipline change answers all four failures plus the budget strain, carried by the Planner, Researcher, and Verifier rubrics. The Planner reserves one engagement-threshold target per confirmed Regulation — the provision that decides whether the regime engages at all — symmetrical with the Applicability targets unconfirmed Regulations already earn (the glossary gains the term alongside this ADR), and lists reserved targets first, so the one-shot truncation correction can never discard them; duty areas compete for the remaining seats as today. The Planner enforces a regime-coverage rule keyed off the question and the description it already reads: every Regulation the Regulatory question or the Scenario's facts name earns at least one target, so a dominant regime can never crowd a named regime out of the plan. The Planner enumerates a model-provider duty area whenever the case names or implies a generative AI model supplied by a vendor — adopting the deferred heuristic on the re-check ADR-0014's accepted-residual-risk clause required, the re-run's vendor-branch drift on both generative cases being that re-check firing. The Researcher cites the provision that defines a term a claim turns on — deciding engagement is not restatement in the abstract — and never drafts a claim whose contingency the Scenario's facts exclude, drafting instead the claim the stated facts support. The Verifier gains the explicit unsupported case — a contingency the Scenario's facts exclude is unsupported, not moderate, while contingencies the Scenario genuinely leaves open keep rating moderate — bringing the shipped rubric into line with the glossary's existing Unsupported-claim definition, the line anchored by one abstract worked example deliberately not drawn from the eval cases (a stated private customer base does not make a company a financial entity, so claims developing the full financial-entity regime under that contingency are unsupported). The declared budget rises from six to eight Research targets on the scored evidence of the re-run — the scored-evidence rule ADR-0012's own retrieval change follows, its recall scores buying the second leg as the re-run's scores buy the seats — with the Evidence pool bound (seats per target × accepted targets) scaling automatically and truncation-correction semantics unchanged.

## Considered options

- **Spread the scope rules across per-case prompt tweaks** — rejected: the failures are systematic, not per-case; rubric-level rules carry to every future plan, and the pinned-phrase tests catch drift loudly.
- **A dedicated structural scope-resolution pipeline stage** — rejected in favour of the rubric-level gate, as in ADR-0014: the Verifier already judges every claim against the Scenario, so a new stage is machinery to revisit only if the re-run still shows excluded branches surviving.
- **Raise the budget without reserving engagement-threshold targets** — rejected: eight loose seats would be spent on duty areas again and the definitional provisions would stay unretrieved; the reservation, not the ceiling, is what buys the threshold citations.
- **Fix the vendor-branch drift in retrieval** (duty-area boosting or a provider-chunk quota) — rejected: retrieval already surfaces provider-side chunks; the miss is the Planner never asking for the vendor-side duty area, so enumeration at planning is the targeted fix and the store read paths stay untouched.

## Consequences

- The three agent-boundary prompts grow; the new rubric phrases are pinned at the scripted-provider seam per prior art, and the declared-budget phrase, the truncation-correction test, and the pool-bound test re-pin to the new limit so budget and rubric drift keep failing the suite.
- The Evidence pool scales with the plan as before, so no target is starved of seats under the raised budget; nothing else in the retrieval, Proposer, Summarizer, scoring, or Demo-mode paths moves.
- The Live eval is re-run manually by the operator under a fresh run id, with directional acceptance: mean coverage precision clearly up, and no Scenario's F1 materially regressed.
- None of these decisions is to be quietly reversed: lowering the budget back to six, dropping the engagement-threshold reservation or the regime-coverage rule, or un-adopting the model-provider heuristic takes a new ADR, not a code change.
